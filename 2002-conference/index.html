Conference

Welcome to ASIC 2002

The First Annual Summer Interdisciplinary Conference (ASIC) was held in Squamish, British Columbia. Richard M. Shiffrin of Indiana University-Bloomington was the conference organizer.

Photographer: Alistair Veitch

This conference was modeled after the Winter AIC conference that had been held for almost 30 years: days are free for leisure activities and the talks are in the later afternoon/early evening. The date was chosen to make it convenient for attendees to bring family and friends. The conference was open to all interested parties, and an invitation was not needed. The subject is interdisciplinary, within the broad frame of Cognitive Science.

Invitation

The conference was open to all scholars who fit the very general theme of the conference, and their family and friends. An individual invitation was not needed — the conference information was freely spread among friends and colleagues.

Conference aims

The conference covered a wide range of subjects in cognitive science, including:

  • neuroscience, cognitive neuroscience
  • psychology (including perception, psychophysics, attention, information processing, memory and cognition)
  • computer science
  • machine intelligence and learning
  • linguistics
  • and philosophy

The conference especially invited talks emphasizing theory, mathematical modeling and computational modeling (including neural networks and artificial intelligence). Talks were required to be comprehensible and interesting to a wide scientific audience, with speakers providing overviews of current research areas as well as their own recent progress.

Conference format

The conference started with a reception on the first evening, Tuesday, July 30, at 5 PM, followed by a partial session.

Each of the next five evenings, the sessions began at 4:30 PM. Drinks, light refreshments and snacks were available starting at 4:15 PM, prior to the start of the session, and at the midway break. A session consisted of 6–7 talks and a mid-session break, finishing at approximately 8:30–8:45 PM. A banquet was held following the final session of the conference.

The conference ended Sunday evening with the banquet. Most attendees used the following day, Monday, August 5, as a travel day.

There were no parallel sessions or presentations. A separate room, day and time was set aside for poster presentations, both for those preferring this format to a spoken presentation and for any presenters who could not be allotted speaking slots.

This conference format freed most of the day for various activities with colleagues, family, and friends.

Travel & Lodging

Squamish is located on Howe Sound, in the southwest corner of British Columbia — about 30 miles north of Vancouver, roughly halfway between Vancouver and the resort city of Whistler.

Most attendees flew into Vancouver International Airport. The scenic drive from the Vancouver airport took approximately 75 minutes, with rental cars, rail, bus, and limo/van service all available from Vancouver. Attendees could also fly to Seattle, though the drive to Squamish from there was substantial; a small airport in Squamish itself had taxi service available.

Lodging

Best Western Sea to Sky Hotel, Garibaldi Highlands, B.C.

The conference hotel was the Best Western Sea to Sky Hotel (40330 Tantulus Way, PO Box 310, Garibaldi Highlands, B.C. V0N 1T0), about 3 km north of the center of Squamish. The hotel had a restaurant, fitness room, sauna and jacuzzi, with several dining choices nearby. ASIC reserved the entire hotel, including a substantial number of standard rooms with two extra-long double beds, as well as several suites.

Activities

Squamish sits amidst mountains at the tip of Howe Sound, near the mouth of the Squamish, Mamquam and other rivers — adjacent to the most famous rock climbing face in Canada, the Stawamus Chief (roughly 2,000 ft. tall).

Climbing at Squamish, adjacent to the Stawamus Chief.

Squamish is most famous for its climbing, windsurfing, fishing and mountain biking, with superb opportunities in many other sports and recreations as well.

Whitewater rafting on the rivers near Squamish.

The 44th Annual Squamish Days Logger Sports competition and festival ran August 1–5, with many activities including world-class logging competitions.

Windsurfing on Howe Sound.

Climbing closures: A voluntary closure was in place on the upper sections of the Stawamus Chief's Grand Wall due to nesting peregrine falcons, expected to lift July 31 once the fledglings learned to fly. The upper pitches of routes from University Wall to Black Dyke were closed; climbing on the Grand Wall was permitted only to the top of the "Sword" pitch. Western Dihedral routes, including Freeway, remained open throughout.
